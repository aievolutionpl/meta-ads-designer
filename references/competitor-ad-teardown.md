# Competitor Ad Teardown

> The operating tool behind **step 2 (Research)** and **R35 (variation matrix)**: turn a competitor's *winning* ad into a structured brief — the angle, hook, layout and format that are already proven in the market — and use the structure as a new starting point, without copying their assets.

Competitors are free research. The ads they keep running (or spent enough to make winners) encode what actually works in your niche. A teardown extracts the **structure**, which is yours to reuse, while leaving their **assets** (photos, logo, likeness) untouched — those stay theirs (R03).

---

## 1 · What a teardown is

A teardown reduces a real ad to its testable DNA:

- the **angle** (Problem / Effect / Lifestyle, R13)
- the **hook** (R36) and the promise it makes (R06)
- the **layout** — hierarchy, ratio, type scale (R07, R19)
- the **format** — static vs. video/UGC, length, ratio (R38, R39)
- the **light/mood** (R09) and the scene (R24)

You are not copying the picture. You are learning *which promise and structure the market rewards* — then re-expressing it for your client.

---

## 2 · Where to find winning ads

- **Meta Ad Library** (adlibrary.facebook.com) — filter by brand/niche and region; ads kept running are the strongest signal.
- **TikTok Creative Center** — top ads by niche.
- **Competitor social feeds** — pinned or repeated content is what they believe in.
- **Client's own winners** — if the client has ads that performed, those are the first and best source of truth for *their* style.

Prefer ads with **volume behind them** (spend / longevity) over one-off flukes.

---

## 3 · The teardown template

For each ad, record:

| Field | What you extract |
|-------|------------------|
| **Brand & niche** | Who, what market |
| **Promise / angle** | The single benefit and the angle (R13, R06) |
| **Hook** | The opening line / first second (R36) |
| **Layout & hierarchy** | Primary → secondary → tertiary; ratio (R07, R19) |
| **Format** | Static / video / UGC; length; captions (R38, R39) |
| **Light & mood** | The photographic character (R09) |
| **Why it likely wins** | Your read on the mechanism |
| **Reusable structure** | What you would test in your own set (R35) |

Fill one row per ad into a small table — 3–5 teardowns give you a read on the niche's conventions and its clichés to avoid (SKILL.md step 2).

---

## 4 · From teardown to brief

1. **Cluster the teardowns** — which angle/hook keeps recurring (that's the market's default; test against it).
2. **Find the gap** — what no one in the niche says; that is your differentiation.
3. **Build a variation matrix** (R35) that tests the strongest proven structure **against** your gap.
4. **Pair with a hook** (R36) and a metaphor that is yours, not theirs.

The teardown becomes the *default row* in your matrix — a proven baseline to beat, not a template to clone.

---

## 5 · Ethics: steal structure, not assets

- **Structure, formulas and angles are fair game.** Real creators and agencies learn this way.
- **Assets are not.** Never reuse a competitor's photo, logo, face, or copy verbatim (R03, R30-logo). Re-express the idea for your client.
- **Respect likeness.** If the winning ad uses a real person, you don't replicate them — you cast your own or use a UGC approach (R39 §3).
- **Flag inspiration, ship original.** A teardown is a research note; the delivered creative is yours.
