# Art direction: turn a marketing idea into a visible decision

Use before writing an image prompt. This is the practical method for R42–R44.
A direction is ready when another designer could sketch it without inventing the hierarchy.

## 1 · The decision card

Resolve these in a short working note, not a long questionnaire:
- Audience and moment: who sees this, and what are they trying to do?
- Offer and evidence: what is actually available? Which supplied fact or image supports it?
- One takeaway: what should the viewer remember?
- Action: what should they do next, and is the destination known?
- Brand: assets, palette, tone, type preferences and visual references.
- Deliverable: prompt only, finished image, or a campaign; placement, ratio and language.

Infer reversible style choices and state important assumptions. Ask only for facts that change the offer or prevent accurate work. Never invent a price, deadline, testimonial, award, address or measurable result. Omit unsupported claims. A concept is a hypothesis, not proof of conversion.

## 2 · Make the benefit visible

Write: “The viewer should understand ___ because they see ___.”
If the second half just says “beautiful product”, the visual has not yet earned its place.

| Marketing job | Visible mechanism | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Desire | One specific appetising detail, tactile material or use moment | Generic gloss on everything |
| Explain utility | A clear action and its consequence in the same scene | Impossible mechanisms or misleading demonstrations |
| Reduce uncertainty | Supplied workmanship detail, actual room or authentic process | Invented proof, fake reviews |
| Announce an event | Event title as hero, date and place grouped below | Atmosphere burying attendance details |
| Promote an offer | Verified offer as dominant type, product providing context | Multiple badges all competing |
| Build recognition | Repeat a distinctive brand shape, crop or type treatment | Borrowing a competitor's identity |

For concept exploration, consider three mechanisms only when useful: literal demonstration, an audience situation, and a graphic or typographic interpretation. Pick the one that communicates best at phone size. For a single requested prompt, deliver the strongest direction directly. A campaign can explore multiple ideas; a controlled test holds other variables fixed.

## 3 · Choose a visual grammar

Do not use the same photo-and-footer construction for every brief. Choose by communication need, source assets and brand character.

| Direction | Composition and type | Best reason to use it | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product sculpture | One large product silhouette, offset placement, quiet type field; compact sans | Shape/material is the selling point | Floating packshot without a believable setting |
| Documentary moment | Tight action crop, natural available light, short type in a calm area | Authentic use or local service | Stock-photo smiles and over-polished skin |
| Editorial | Deliberate asymmetry, generous margins, restrained serif/sans contrast | Considered hospitality, culture, craftsmanship | Calling every business luxury |
| Typographic poster | Headline or event name owns the frame; image optional; one strong alignment | Announcement or offer | Tiny practical information below enormous decoration |
| Graphic reduction | One purposeful silhouette, illustration or flat shape; limited palette | An idea is clearer as a symbol | Random icons and meaningless geometry |
| Expressive flyer | Large condensed type, controlled overlap, one rhythmic graphic device | Music, events, energetic retail | Distressed texture destroying letterforms |
| Photo and panel | Image and information have separate fields; shared alignment and palette | Busy source photography or exact detail copy | Identical 60/40 templates across every campaign |

A flat graphic needs no lens, depth of field or photographic shadows. A documentary image needs no mandatory premium polish. Cream, purple, gradients and illustration are legitimate when the brand and idea justify them. Reject arbitrary use, not the medium or colour.

## 4 · Specify the frame

Use normalized coordinates or approximate percentages as composition intent; a generative model cannot guarantee exact geometry.

Record:
- Canvas and reading path: e.g. headline → subject → action.
- Dominant element: position, approximate occupied area and silhouette.
- Copy area: its position, maximum lines and alignment.
- Quiet area: what stays absent so the focal element can breathe.
- Brand anchor and practical information: location and relative prominence.
- Edge treatment: bleed for imagery; protected space for critical content.
- Crop strategy: show the whole product when identity requires it; use intentional close-ups when detail is the idea.

Check that the described regions can coexist. Never put both the hero and a text block in the same “empty lower third”. A useful starting point is a dominant field occupying around half to two-thirds of the frame, but no percentage is a universal quality rule. Use the layout reference for production starting values, then fit actual copy and assets.

## 5 · Direct typography and colour

Choose by the shape of the words, language, brand and available space:
- A condensed face fits short, energetic headlines; a broad heavy face creates blunt emphasis; a high-contrast serif suits editorial pacing.
- Name a preferred family AND describe its role, weight and width. A name alone does not control generated typography.
- Start with one display family and one support family. Use fewer when sufficient.
- Specify the exact line breaks and copy strings. Establish dominant headline, supporting detail, then action. Do not shrink essential information merely to preserve an arbitrary ratio.
- Specify palette roles: background, text, accent. Use the brand's palette before category presets.
- Use emphasis once: scale, weight or colour at the key message. Multiple equal emphases cancel hierarchy.
- Put text on a deliberately calm field. Overlay is valid when contrast and image structure support it.

Exact font files, brand colour values, logos, diacritics, prices and dense event details require a separate composition step when the image tool cannot preserve them reliably. Describe that handoff clearly; a prompt cannot guarantee a renderer's spelling or font implementation.

## 6 · The taste critique

Before delivery, answer with observable evidence:
1. Message: can someone say what is advertised without reading a paragraph?
2. Eye path: what is seen first, second, third?
3. Distinction: what decision makes this belong to this brand?
4. Coherence: do type, image, colour and spacing speak the same visual language?
5. Restraint: which removable element did you cut?
6. Small size: do the subject and headline remain identifiable at phone scale?

For a prompt, check whether these are specified. For a rendered image, inspect whether they actually happened. Never give a rendered-image QA score to a prompt. Fix the weakest decision instead of appending “more premium, more beautiful, 8K”.
