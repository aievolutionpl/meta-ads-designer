# Video & UGC Motion Track

> The operating tool behind **R39**: the rules of static advertising survive into motion, and motion is chosen for the job it does — never to decorate. Product-image-to-video, UGC/avatar, motion from stills, animated loops — each has a purpose, a model, and a QA.

Most creative that wins today is motion. But motion is a **layer on a design that already works**, not a way to rescue a weak static. The hero frame of a video must pass the same gate as a static ad (R02–R34).

---

## 1 · When the brief needs motion

Reach for the motion track when:

- The promise is **demonstrated** (how it works, what it does) — motion shows function that a still can't (R12).
- The audience is **short-form native** (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) — the format expects motion (R19).
- You want **UGC credibility** — a talking-head or product-review feel.
- The hook (R36) **needs a beat** — a reveal, a transformation, a moment.

If the promise is a single strong still (a dish, a room, a product hero), a static ad is often the right call — don't add motion because it's fashionable.

---

## 2 · Motion formats

| Format | What it is | Use for |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Product-image-to-video** | Animate a still product photo — camera move, light, subtle motion | Hero shots, product focus |
| **UGC / avatar talking-head** | A presenter (real or AI avatar) sells the product | Reviews, demos, credibility |
| **Motion from stills** | 3–12s edits animating several stills | Sequences, before/after, story |
| **Animated loop** | A self-looping moment | Awareness, feed repeat views |
| **Scroll-stopper beat** | One strong motion moment | The first second (R36) |

Pick the format for the **promise** (R35), not for novelty.

---

## 3 · UGC / avatar route

- **UGC style** is a production mode, not a quality licence: still commercial realism (R04), a real or realistic presenter, purposeful on-screen copy — never a HUD (R05).
- **The first second carries the message** (R36): the hook is spoken or on-screen within the first beat.
- **On-screen text is large, clear, purposeful** — readable on a small screen at full playback speed (R33).
- **Respect likeness and reference** (R03): a real client/founder keeps their identity; a supplied presenter photo is source of truth.

---

## 4 · Static-first thinking still applies

Every rule that governs a still governs the video's frames:

- Product is the main character in the hero frame (R02).
- One idea per video (R06) — a 15-second demo of one benefit beats a montage of seven.
- Hierarchy survives motion: what the eye should land on is clear even when the frame moves (R07).
- No AI slop (R05): no random glitch, hologram, or generator-default motion effects.

---

## 5 · Model routing for video

Match the generator to the motion job — full table in [`model-routing.md`](model-routing.md):

- **Precise motion / product demo** → a model strong at controlled camera and motion.
- **Cinematic / story-driven** → a model with smooth multi-scene transitions.
- **Audio-led / UGC talking-head** → a model with lip-sync and natural speech.
- **Motion from stills** → frame-accurate editing of the stills you already QA'd.

Whatever the model, **QA the hero frame and the first second** (R34, R36) before you render the full length — a bad first frame wastes the whole render.
