# Hook & Headline Engineering

> The operating tool behind **R36**: the hook is decided before the visual, and it is engineered — not improvised. This is the "20→3 gate" that keeps a creative from being a pretty picture with a placeholder headline.

The first second (video) or the thumbnail (static) decides whether the ad is seen at all. The hook is therefore the most important copy you write, and it has to be **decided first** — the scene is built around it, not the reverse (R24, R17).

---

## 1 · Why the hook is decided first

If you design the image and then search for words to paste on it, you have already lost: the composition was built for the wrong message. The hook and the visual must be **one idea** (R06). Resolve in words *what stops the scroll*, then build the picture that carries that same promise.

---

## 2 · The 20→3 gate

Do not settle for the first good line. Run the gate:

1. **Write 20 candidate hooks** for the one promise. Quantity first, quality later — the first five are usually the obvious ones, the value is in 6–20.
2. **Rank** them by stop-power and how tightly they fit the target's situation (not by how clever they sound).
3. **Pick 3 structurally different survivors** — they must differ by *opening mechanism*, not by three phrasings of the same line.

Three survivors → three cells in the variation matrix (R35). The other 17 are not wasted — they are the next set's raw material.

---

## 3 · Hook formulas that work

Opening mechanisms that reliably stop the scroll — pick a different one per survivor:

- **Problem open** — the frustration *before* the product. *"Reaching the top shelf means a step ladder."* (R13)
- **Result open** — the outcome up front. *"Every spice at eye level."* (R13)
- **Question / open loop** — curiosity that needs closing. *"Why does this kitchen look effortless?"*
- **Specific number** — concrete and credible. *"3 seconds to drop the shelf."*
- **Objection kill** — the reason people skip, answered. *"No step ladder. No stretching."*
- **Belonging** — the target sees themselves. *"For people who cook every day."*
- **Negation** — the anti-slop counter to the obvious. *"Not another gadget. A fix."*

Keep it **short and scannable** — the thumb-read of a feed or the first second of a reel. See the character budgets in [`headline-system.md`](headline-system.md).

---

## 4 · Character & diacritic budgets

- **Static headline:** keep it short enough to read at thumbnail size (R33) — headline-system budgets apply.
- **Video hook:** readable in the first second; the caption must survive small-screen playback.
- **Diacritics (PL and other languages):** every accented letter must be correct — a missing `ż` or `ł` is a spelling fail (R30-text). Check the final render, not the prompt.

---

## 5 · Hook ↔ visual metaphor pairing

Each surviving hook is paired with a **visual metaphor** that makes it literal:

| Hook | Paired visual metaphor |
|------|------------------------|
| Problem open | The frustrating *before* moment, clearly readable (R24) |
| Result open | The satisfying *after* moment, benefit in frame (R12) |
| Specific number | The moment that proves the number (e.g. shelf at the right height) |
| Objection kill | The product doing what the audience doubted |

The pair is then fed to the creative workflow as one unit — you are never choosing an image and a headline independently again.
