# Static ad formats: the persuasion skeletons

> The operating tool behind **R46**. A format is the structure that persuades: where the claim sits, what proves it and how the eye reaches the action. Choose the format from the proof you actually have and the audience's stage, then dress it in a style from [style-atlas-2026.md](style-atlas-2026.md).

Static images still carry a large share of Meta conversions, and third-party analyses of 2026 ads keep finding the same few structures behind winning statics. One analysis of static layouts reports that structured templates such as quote cards, stat drops and product callouts beat plain product shots. Treat such figures as direction, not a promised lift.

---

## 1 · Pick by proof, then by funnel

**The proof rule.** Every format needs a specific kind of evidence. If the brief lacks it, the format is not available. Never manufacture the proof to fit the template (R43).

| Format | Proof it needs |
|---|---|
| Stat drop | A verified number with a source |
| Review card / review stack | Real customer words, with permission where needed |
| Founder / expert quote | A real person who said it and agrees to be shown |
| Comparison / us vs them | Verifiable, fair differences |
| Before / after | Real, unedited results, allowed in the category |
| Offer stack | The actual offer terms |
| Product + callouts | True product features |
| Bold statement, native interface, problem → solution | A clear position; no external proof, but no false claims |

**Funnel fit.**

| Stage | Viewer state | Formats that fit |
|---|---|---|
| Cold | Does not know the brand or problem framing | Bold statement · native interface · problem → solution · stat drop · reality-warp metaphor |
| Warm | Knows the problem, compares options | Product + callouts · comparison · review stack · numbered benefits · founder quote |
| Hot / retargeting | Considered it, needs a reason now | Offer stack · review card · verified deadline · objection kill |

## 2 · Canvas zones

All skeletons below use the 4:5 canvas (1080×1350 design math, export 1440×1800 where the placement recommends it) and the 86px margin from [layout-system.md](layout-system.md). "Upper band" means roughly the top third, "hero field" the middle, "base" the lowest band. These are composition intent, not pixel promises. Re-lay out for 9:16: critical copy stays in the central band between 14% from the top and 35% from the bottom ([platform-compliance.md](platform-compliance.md)).

## 3 · The formats

Each entry: skeleton → copy budget → where it shines → integrity rule.

### 3.1 · Bold statement

- **Skeleton.** Headline fills the upper band and hero field (2–4 lines). Small product or brand cue in the base. Optional single image behind or beside.
- **Copy.** Headline ≤ 40 characters. No subline, or one short line.
- **Shines.** Cold audiences, positioning, launches, brands with a strong opinion.
- **Integrity.** A strong claim still has to be true; "the best" without proof is out.

### 3.2 · Stat drop

- **Skeleton.** One number at display size (the largest element), a one-line label beneath it, product or context image supporting in the base or at the side, source line in legal size.
- **Copy.** Number + ≤ 6-word label, for example "1,400 stoves fitted on this island".
- **Shines.** Supplements and beauty (clinical or customer numbers), services (volume, years, response time), apps (users).
- **Integrity.** The number is verified and its source is on file. Round honestly and never round up past the truth.

### 3.3 · Review card

- **Skeleton.** One quotation as the headline (quote marks as a graphic device), attribution under it (first name, context), rating or source mark if real, product in the base or beside.
- **Copy.** Quote ≤ 90 characters. Cut the review; do not rewrite its meaning.
- **Shines.** Mid-funnel trust, food and beverages, local services, beauty.
- **Integrity.** Real review, real reviewer, verbatim or faithfully shortened. Never invent names, stars or platform badges.

### 3.4 · Review stack

- **Skeleton.** Three short real reviews as cards or chat-like bubbles, staggered down the frame, product anchored in one corner, one line naming the review source.
- **Copy.** Each review ≤ 60 characters; together they cover three different reasons to buy.
- **Shines.** Food and beverages, e-commerce, hospitality, retargeting.
- **Integrity.** As for the review card. Three reviews saying the same thing are one review.

### 3.5 · Product + callouts

- **Skeleton.** Product large at the centre or offset, 3–5 short callouts at the edges connected by thin or hand-drawn arrows, headline in the upper band, CTA or brand cue in the base.
- **Copy.** Callouts ≤ 3 words each. Four is the sweet spot; seven overwhelms.
- **Shines.** Tech, tools, apparel with construction details, skincare ingredients, multi-feature products.
- **Integrity.** The product stays dominant; callouts that outshout it defeat the format. Every callout is a true feature. Keep the product identical to the reference (R03).

### 3.6 · Comparison grid / us vs them

- **Skeleton.** Two columns: "us" (brand colour, product) and "the usual way" (neutral grey), 3–4 rows of ticks and crosses or short phrases, headline above.
- **Copy.** Row labels ≤ 4 words.
- **Shines.** Home, tech, tools, subscriptions, services replacing a DIY habit.
- **Integrity.** Compare with a category or habit ("store-bought", "the old way") unless a named comparison is verified and legally cleared. Differences must be fair and checkable.

### 3.7 · Before / after split

- **Skeleton.** Two states side by side or top and bottom with a clean divider and identical framing, small labels, headline naming the change.
- **Copy.** Labels "Before" / "After" or the time between; headline ≤ 30 characters.
- **Shines.** Cleaning, renovation, furniture, landscaping, organisation, apparel fit.
- **Integrity.** Real, same-subject, same-angle, unedited results. Health, weight-loss and some beauty claims are restricted in Meta's advertising standards. Where they are, use R22's problem → effect pair or a process image instead.

### 3.8 · Numbered benefit list

- **Skeleton.** Headline with a number ("3 reasons…"), a numbered list with large numerals as the visual rhythm, product at the side or in the base.
- **Copy.** Items ≤ 5 words each; 3 items preferred, 5 maximum.
- **Shines.** Education, services, SaaS, anything with several decisive reasons.
- **Integrity.** Each item is a separate true benefit, not the same one reworded.

### 3.9 · Founder / expert quote

- **Skeleton.** Real portrait (documentary light, not a stock smile), a first-person line as the headline, name and role, small brand cue.
- **Copy.** Quote ≤ 80 characters, in the person's own voice.
- **Shines.** Food and beverage brands, craft businesses, clinics, professional services, local businesses.
- **Integrity.** Real person, real statement, consent to appear. Never a generated "founder".

### 3.10 · Offer stack

- **Skeleton.** Product or bundle in the hero field, the offer as the dominant type (sticker or large numeral), 2–3 lines of what is included, deadline or condition in the base.
- **Copy.** Offer ≤ 20 characters; conditions short but complete.
- **Shines.** Retargeting, seasonal sales, bundles, first-order offers.
- **Integrity.** Every term is verified, including price, percentage, deadline and stock. False urgency is a hard fail (R43).

### 3.11 · Native interface (notes, chat, post)

- **Skeleton.** The anatomy of a familiar screen (notes list, chat thread, post, sticky note) filling the frame, one highlighted line, product or brand cue small in a corner. Build deterministically (Mode B).
- **Copy.** As long as it takes to read in 3 seconds: a title plus 3–5 short lines.
- **Shines.** Confessions ("things I wish I knew…"), objection handling, founder notes, services, apps.
- **Integrity.** Brand's own voice, clearly the brand's own content. No fake tappable UI (play buttons, close X, notification badges), no impersonation of a real person, platform account or news outlet.

### 3.12 · Problem → solution

- **Skeleton.** Either a single frame showing the frustration with the solution entering, or a pair of full-size creatives (R13, R22). Headline names the problem the audience recognises.
- **Copy.** Problem headline ≤ 35 characters, solution line ≤ 30.
- **Shines.** Cold audiences, services, tools, household products.
- **Integrity.** Do not exaggerate the problem with fake damage or manipulated "before" images.

### 3.13 · Checkerboard

- **Skeleton.** A 2×2 grid alternating copy and imagery: headline tile, product tile, benefit tile, in-use tile. One shared palette and alignment.
- **Copy.** One idea per text tile, ≤ 6 words.
- **Shines.** Retail, apparel, home goods, restaurants showing dish, place, people and offer.
- **Integrity.** A designed grid is intentional; the QA script's collage heuristic may flag it. Declare it and judge it by eye. The collage check exists to catch *unintended* batch images, several ads returned in one render, which stay a failure.

### 3.14 · Advertorial static

- **Skeleton.** Editorial headline in a serif, documentary photo, short deck line, small "Sponsored"-style brand label. Reads like a feature page, clearly from the brand.
- **Copy.** Headline ≤ 60 characters, deck ≤ 90.
- **Shines.** Considered purchases, health-adjacent products, B2B, education.
- **Integrity.** Never imitate a real publication's masthead or pretend to be independent journalism.

### 3.15 · Hand-held / in-use

- **Skeleton.** A real hand holding or using the product in context, product sharp and large, short headline in a calm area.
- **Copy.** Headline ≤ 30 characters.
- **Shines.** Almost every physical product; beverages, cosmetics, gadgets, food to go.
- **Integrity.** Hands must be anatomically correct (R30). Product identical to the reference.

## 4 · Format by vertical

Starting points, combining third-party 2026 format analyses with this repo's playbooks. The brief can overrule them.

| Vertical | Lead formats | Also test |
|---|---|---|
| Beauty & supplements | Stat drop · review card | Product + callouts · founder quote |
| Apparel & accessories | Hand-held / in-use · product + callouts | Before / after fit · checkerboard |
| Home & furniture | Before / after · comparison | Checkerboard · review stack |
| Food & beverage | Review stack · founder quote | Hand-held · seasonal offer stack |
| Restaurants & cafés | Real dish hero (hospitality playbook) · review card | Founder quote · offer stack |
| Hotels & venues | Real-place hero · review card | Advertorial · bold statement |
| Local services & trades | Documentary in-use · review card · problem → solution | Stat drop (years, jobs) · native interface |
| Tech, tools & apps | Product + callouts · comparison | Device-in-hand · stat drop |
| Real estate | Device-in-hand · real property hero | Stat drop (m², minutes to…) · checkerboard |
| Education & courses | Numbered benefits · founder / expert quote | Native interface · bold statement |
| Events | Bold statement (type-led) | Seasonal world · stat drop (line-up size) |

## 5 · Hooks that pair with formats

Hooks reported among 2026's most-used or most efficient for large advertisers (Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026, BrandMov's hand-tagged DTC analysis). The hook is decided before the visual (R36); this table suggests which skeleton gives each hook its strongest shape.

| Hook | Mechanism | Best format |
|---|---|---|
| Confession | "I used to…", an admitted mistake | Native interface · founder quote |
| Bold claim | One strong position | Bold statement · stat drop |
| Relatability | "If you… you know" | Problem → solution · native interface |
| Contrast | Old way vs new way | Comparison · before / after |
| Curiosity | An open loop | Bold statement · reality warp |
| Sale | Verified offer first | Offer stack |
| In-30-seconds | Speed of result or setup | Numbered benefits · product + callouts |
| Ever tried / noticed | Names a shared experience | Problem → solution · review card |
| Feature breakdown | One differentiating feature | Product + callouts |

## 6 · Carousel note

For carousels, the first card must work as a complete static ad; many viewers see only one or two cards. Sequence the rest as a story: hook → context → proof → reward → action. Three to five cards are usually enough. When the order matters, turn off automatic "best-performing card first" ordering so the platform does not reshuffle the story. Keep one style across all cards.
